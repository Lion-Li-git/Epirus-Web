This is the only such case.

## Top 10 most concerning findings

1. **D57 documents a gate that was vacuous for ~100 versions — and the repo only found it by accident.** `np:1392-1394`: *"这里原来钉的是 `passiveFieldAt` 的三条正向断言 … 而那条注入自 v1.5.65 起一局没开过火（消费点查 `BOT_PICKS` 不存在的键）。键与分支都已删除，断言随之反向：不许复活。"* A gate asserting `passiveFieldAt(...) === true` was green for the entire life of a feature whose injection point never fired once. What could go wrong: the same shape is still live elsewhere — every "the wiring exists" assertion in the suite (D11, D13, D74, D84-89, D114, D118-132) checks the *presence of a hook*, and D57 is the proof that a present hook can be a dead one. D122's `EPIRUS_KILL_FIELD` "空枪检测" (firing counts, `np:4530-4534`) is the right antidote; it has not been applied to the other ~20 knob gates.

2. **D82 is a live near-tautology, and the comment says it replaced an earlier always-true gate.** `np:3492-3499`: when no `.bak` is younger than 24 h, the case does `ok(cd.length > 1000, 'CHANGELOG 必须非空…')` and returns. `CHANGELOG.md` is 307,265 bytes, so the assertion cannot fail short of truncating the ledger, and the gate's stated job — naming freshly-produced artifacts — is not performed at all. What could go wrong: an unrecorded night of 20 artifacts (the exact §7-5 incident) passes unnoticed whenever the gate runs outside the 24 h window, and inside the window it is the *only* thing catching it.

3. **Two gates silently skip their only real measurement when a fixture is unreadable (D58, D59).** `np:1455-1457` and `np:1502-1504` catch a parse/read failure, print `（跳过…：线上包此刻不可读，多半是训练中）`, and fall through to `t()`'s PASS. D59 is the expensive one: it runs `T.mirrorHealth(live, 400, 5, 'multi')` and asserts the incumbent's 5-seat spread `< 40` — the guard against the v1.5.114 "43pt red" recurrence. What could go wrong: a malformed or truncated `js/bundled-champion-3p.js` — precisely the artifact hand-edited by promotion tooling — turns a 400-game seat-bias check into a printed note and a green tick. The suite only reveals this to someone reading stdout; our run printed no skip line, so it is invisible in the normal "188/188" summary.

4. **D67's `--force` exception validator is dead code while the incumbent passes.** `np:3061-3084`: `ok(refPassG4 || g4recOk, …)` short-circuits on `refPassG4`. Today's `js/bundled-champion-3p.js` carries `"gate4Forced":{"forced":false,...,"lines":[]}` — so the entire `implOk`/`g4recOk` apparatus (including the frozen `G4IMPL_AT_EXCEPTION = 'caecc92f'` migration crutch at `np:3069`) is never evaluated. What could go wrong: the day the incumbent actually fails G4 is the *first* day that exception logic ever runs in anger, in the very commit that is trying to ship a champion. There is also a copy risk: `caecc92f` is a hash of `tools/gate-drafts.mjs` chooser source + `pT`, maintained by hand in two files.

5. **D12's structural prohibition has a hole it does not see.** `np:1116` forbids the literal substring `B[BOT_FN_N[`; `server/train-server.mjs:379` still declares `const BOT_FN_N = OPP_FN;` and `server/train-server.mjs:450` still feeds it to `makeOppSelResolver(sb, root, BOT_FN_N, sb.EpirusBots)`. The gate's own comment describes the failure as "加名字漏一处", so the *name→function mapping* being centralized (through `makeOppSelResolver`) satisfies the spirit; but the assertion tests spelling, not structure. What could go wrong: re-introducing the exact v1.5.2 bug as `B[ BOT_FN_N[ nm ] ]`, or via any other identifier/path, passes this gate silently.

## Gates that could NOT be fully classified, and why

None were left unclassified — every D-gate received exactly one primary bucket. The following 7 are marked with a **verification limit**, meaning the bucket is decided on the code as it stands but a specific claim inside them could not be confirmed from source alone:

1. **D16** — the "training must not write the live bundle" branch (`np:1527-1531`) requires `docs/artifacts/.training.lock` + `.training-live.sha1`, which do not exist outside a training run. We classified it ACTIVE on the strength of the always-executed fingerprint half and recorded the other half as a dead-in-normal-operation branch; **we could not observe the branch executing** because the task forbids running training.
2. **D58** — the 5-seat hogging statistics are fixture-conditional. Our run printed no skip line, which implies the bundle parsed and the branch ran, but the case emits nothing identifying that fact, so we cannot *prove* from the output alone that the 60-game measurement executed (only that the skip message was absent).
3. **D59** — same structure; same limitation. Additionally `SEAT_N = 400` is a gate-local literal (`np:1495`) with no counterpart in `js/train/evo.js` (verified: no `SEAT_N` there), so we could not determine whether 400 is duplicated elsewhere in the tooling or is genuinely single-source in the gate.
4. **D67** — the exception validator cannot be exercised while the incumbent passes, and forcing it (`--force`) is a write operation outside this read-only audit.
5. **D105 / D109 / D127** — the V2/V4/shape scores and the S4_W scorer are measured against the *live* champion pack and external lines; we read the thresholds and the assertions but did not re-derive the numbers, so "the value is currently meaningful" is taken from the passing run rather than independently recomputed.
6. **D123 / D127** — the byte-identity hash `aa743488cc` was read but not recomputed (recomputing requires running the trainer).
7. **D141** — the two pinned zeros (`wall.by[0].deal === 0`, `wall.by[0].alive === 0`) are re-measured on every run against whatever the incumbent is; we verified the paired non-zero guard exists (`np:5527`) but cannot say whether the zeros are a stable property of the incumbent or a coincidence of this pack revision.

Also worth recording, though not a classification gap: **the suite spawns 31 subprocesses** (`grep -n "spawnSync(" tools/np-test.mjs`, lines 3039, 3324, 4428–5721) with per-call timeouts up to 900,000 ms (`np:5364`, `np:5516`). Roughly 25 of those are real trainer/CLI arm runs (D116, D119, D122–D132, D134–D139). The suite's green result therefore also means "25 CLI arm invocations exited with the exact expected codes" — a much stronger claim than the D-family table alone conveys, and the reason a green suite here is not cheap evidence of gate *quality*: many of the most brittle pins are in those arms' stdout regexes, not in the D-numbered assertions themselves.
